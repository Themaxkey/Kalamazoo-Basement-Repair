Portage grew mostly after the war, and that shows up in its basements. Where central Kalamazoo has block and stone walls laid before anyone was thinking about lateral soil loads, most Portage houses have poured concrete — which fails differently and, on the whole, more forgivingly.

## What That Means For Diagnosis

A poured wall cracks vertically or diagonally, usually from a corner or a window opening, and usually because the concrete shrank as it cured. Those cracks leak, and they are the cheapest serious repair in the trade — [injection](/foundation-crack-repair/) commonly runs $400–$900 per crack.

What a poured wall rarely does is the thing block walls do: crack along a horizontal mortar course and start moving inward. So a [horizontal crack](/horizontal-crack-in-basement-wall/) in a Portage basement is less common, and correspondingly more worth taking seriously when it does appear, because a poured wall has to be under real load before it does that.

## The Actual Portage Problem

Most of what we get called out to here is not the wall at all. It is water arriving from outside and the house having no route to get rid of it.

Post-war subdivisions were graded once, at build, and sixty years of settlement, landscaping, patios, extensions and re-turfing have quietly undone that grading on a lot of lots. Soil settles against a foundation and ends up sloping back toward it. A patio or driveway gets laid shedding the wrong way. Downspouts get shortened when someone lays a border.

The result is [water on the basement floor](/water-in-basement/) within an hour of heavy rain, which is the signature of surface water rather than groundwater. That distinction matters enormously to the bill: the surface version is often corrected outside for a few hundred dollars, while the groundwater version is interior drainage at $8,000–$15,000.

Anyone who quotes you the second without checking the first has skipped a step.

## Sump Pumps

A good share of Portage houses were built with a sump already in place, which is useful and also a trap. A pump that has sat for fifteen years is a pump that may not start, and a system that worked fine when the house was new may have silted tile by now.

Before accepting any large quote, lift the sump lid and pour a bucket of water in. If nothing happens you have a $400–$1,200 problem rather than a five-figure one. [Sump pump repair](/sump-pump-repair/) covers what usually fails and in what order.
