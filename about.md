## How This Site Works

Kalamazoo Basement Repair connects homeowners across Kalamazoo County and the surrounding area with local, licensed and insured basement waterproofing and foundation contractors. When you call the number on this site or send a request through the form, your details go to a local contractor who contacts you directly to arrange an inspection.

We would rather say that plainly here than bury it in a footer. You are not being routed through a call center, and your details are not sold on to a list of competing companies who then all ring you at dinner time.

## What We Care About Getting Right

**Diagnosis before quotation.** The most expensive mistake in this trade is buying the wrong repair. Interior drainage will not fix a wall that is moving. Carbon fiber will not stop water. A crack injection on a crack that is still opening is money spent twice. Which problem you have is readable from the pattern, and that assessment should happen before any number is discussed.

**Outside before inside.** A large share of the water that ends up on a Kalamazoo basement floor gets there because of a downspout discharging at the wall, ground that has settled into a slope running back toward the house, or a window well with a blocked drain. Correcting those costs a fraction of interior work, and on a meaningful number of houses it is the entire fix. Any proposal that goes straight to the expensive option without looking at the drainage has skipped the cheap answer.

**Telling people when they need nothing.** Some floor cracking is concrete curing and always was. Some wall cracks stopped moving decades ago. Some damp is condensation in August rather than water coming in. If that is what your basement shows, you should be told so, and it should not cost you anything to find out.

## What We Will Not Do

We will not price structural work over the phone as though it were a fixed product. We will not tell you a basement can be made permanently free of mold, because spores are present in all air — what can be controlled is moisture. We will not claim a bowed wall can always be pushed back to plumb, because on many walls the finishes and the mortar will not tolerate it and stabilizing in place is the honest answer.

## Why Kalamazoo

The geology here makes this work more common than in most of the country. Glacial ground that changes character street to street, a water table that rises with the thaw, a river valley running through the middle of the city, and a housing stock old enough that many of its foundation walls are unreinforced block laid before anyone was thinking about lateral loads.

On top of that sits the calendar. Ground freezes, stops absorbing, then thaws while snowmelt and spring rain arrive together. Basement problems in this city are not bad luck. They are a predictable consequence of the ground and the season, which is also why they are worth understanding rather than panicking about.
